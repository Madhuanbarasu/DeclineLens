# Problem Statement

**Track 01: Money, Risk & Regulation** (example: "Explaining a declined application to a customer")

## Who has the problem

* **Applicants** who are told "declined" with no usable explanation and no idea what to change.
* **Lenders** (banks, NBFCs, digital lenders) who must explain decisions clearly, keep an audit trail, and show that their decisioning does not quietly disadvantage a group.
* **Compliance and risk teams** who today answer these questions one case at a time, with analysts and spreadsheets.

## Why it matters

* A decline without a reason erodes trust and drives complaints and escalations.
* Reasons alone are not enough. People want to know *what would change the outcome*, and giving that advice responsibly means testing alternatives against the lender's own model rather than guessing.
* Fairness is usually checked only on approval rates. But a lender can approve groups at similar rates and still make it far harder for one group to recover from a decline. That is invisible unless recourse itself is measured.
* Doing all this for every declined applicant is a data-scale problem: each declined applicant must be re-scored under hundreds of alternative scenarios.

## What DeclineLens does

For every declined applicant, inside **Exasol Personal**:

1. Gives the **top three reasons**, computed exactly from the model.
2. Finds the **lowest-effort combination of changes** (smaller loan, longer tenure, lower utilisation, paying down EMIs, building score, waiting out enquiries) that would flip the decision, by re-scoring hundreds of what-if plans per applicant.
3. Produces a **plain-language letter** the customer can act on.
4. **Audits** approval rates, four-fifths impact ratio, the share of declined applicants with *no* path to approval, and the effort of the easiest path, across groups.

## Outcome

Lenders move from a black-box "declined" to an explanation and a tested route to approval for every applicant, with a built-in check on whether that route is fair. Applicants get actionable guidance. Compliance teams get evidence rather than anecdotes.
